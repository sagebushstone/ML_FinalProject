Music streaming platforms including Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music produce extensive quantities of audio data each day, driving an increasing demand for advanced systems capable of accurately categorizing, recommending, and analyzing music at scale. Central to these systems is the task of analyzing the correlations between a song’s auditory characteristics—such as danceability, loudness, energy, acousticness, and tempo—and its associated genre or perceptual classification. Although contemporary recommendation systems utilize sophisticated machine learning and deep learning techniques, examining fundamental audio patterns remains a crucial initial stage in understanding the intrinsic clustering of track attributes and assessing their correspondence with genre classifications.

This project examines the grouping of Spotify track features through unsupervised learning and investigates whether naturally occurring clusters derived from audio features align with genre-based segmentation. Utilizing the Spotify Tracks Dataset (Kaggle), the project implements K-Means clustering, assesses cluster validity through Elbow and Silhouette techniques, visualizes relationships among principal audio features, and compares genre-specific averages for energy and danceability. These procedures offer insight into whether genres—traditionally subjective and fluid—correspond with quantifiable acoustic features.

Furthermore, the project's emphasis on clustering and feature relationships advances the broader domain of Music Information Retrieval (MIR), where aural features underpin genre classification, playlist creation, and recommendation systems. By examining track similarities based on quantifiable attributes, this study not only emphasizes patterns within musical structure but also highlights the challenges and inconsistencies associated with genre labeling, demonstrating whether genres function as distinct clusters or exhibit considerable overlap in feature space. The insights gained establish a foundation for future classification models and enhance our comprehension of feature-based music categorization.
